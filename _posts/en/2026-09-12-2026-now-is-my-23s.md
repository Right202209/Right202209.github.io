> You up ahead, don't forget yourself. Growing up is forever; parting is suspended in midair. — Khalil Fong, "Only 23"

The first time I listened to Khalil Fong's "Only 23," I was already 22. Not exactly young anymore, but listening to the lyrics, I'd still secretly think: I'm not 23 yet, and yet I found myself quietly wondering what changes 23 might bring.

In the blink of an eye, I've actually turned 23. This is also my first birthday blog post.

I used to think birthdays were just another ordinary page on the calendar, with no particular reason they had to be recorded. But so much happened during the year I was 22—if I don't write something now while I can, it feels like time will really just push me forward without pause. I want to properly settle the beginning of my 23rd year here.

Looking back on the past year, it seems I've been saying goodbye to different things all along.

In the summer, I graduated from university. It wasn't the grand farewell I'd imagined—more like the daze that comes after packing up luggage, checking out of the dorm, and watching familiar people head off in different directions.

I used to think "growth" was such a grand word, until I actually stepped out of campus and realized it's something concrete and fragmented—it's starting to make decisions on your own, getting used to days without standard answers, and on some suddenly quiet evening, realizing that you really do have to stand on your own now.

Also left behind in 22 is a relationship that ended.

The line in the song—"parting is suspended in midair"—I used to think it was just beautifully written. Only after truly experiencing it did I understand: that feeling isn't a dramatic collapse, but rather a part of life you'd grown accustomed to suddenly being left hanging in emptiness.

Two people going from talking about everything to retreating back into their separate lives—there was pulling and pushing in between, there was regret, but by the end, what remained was more of a calm letting go.

Some roads can only be walked together for a stretch, and ending doesn't mean the past was meaningless. Even now, I'm still learning to get used to this quiet. Taking back the attention I used to give to another person and placing it back on myself.

Grateful for the road we once walked together—it's just that the scenery ahead needs to be seen separately now.

23 hasn't given me a standard answer, but it's given me a clean sheet of draft paper. I hope this year, I can still keep my curiosity about the world, allow everything to happen, and allow everything to leave.

Me up ahead, don't forget yourself. Happy birthday.
